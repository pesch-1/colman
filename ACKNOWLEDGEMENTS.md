# Acknowledgements

Every piece of software stands on a huge foundation of earlier work.
colman is no exception: the code in this repository is only the topmost,
very thin layer on top of decades of work by countless people, most of
whom gave their work away freely.  This page is a thank-you to all of
them.  It cannot be complete, and it is not meant to be: whoever is
missing here is thanked just as much.

The formal list of third-party material and its licenses is in
[NOTICE](NOTICE).

## The operating system and its tools

- **Linux** — Linus Torvalds and the thousands of kernel developers who
  have built and maintained it since 1991.
- **The GNU project** — Richard Stallman, the Free Software Foundation
  and all contributors to the GNU tools: bash, coreutils, grep, sed,
  make, gdb and many more, and to the GNU General Public License under
  which colman itself is published.
- **Debian**, **Raspberry Pi OS** and all other distributions, whose
  maintainers package, test and integrate all of this so that it simply
  works.

## Compilers and build tools

- **GCC** — the GNU Compiler Collection, which has translated every line
  of colman's C++ code.
- **LLVM/Clang** — in particular `clang-format`, which keeps the code in
  shape.
- **CMake**, **git** and **Doxygen**, without which building, versioning
  and documenting colman would be far more tedious.

## The editor

- **GNU Emacs** — and the large community around it: the authors of
  tree-sitter and its grammars, of `straight.el`, of the `clang-format`
  integration and of the many other packages that turn an editor into a
  home for programming.

## Libraries and frameworks

- **Qt** — the Qt Project and its many contributors, for a framework that
  makes a portable desktop application written in C++ possible.
- **SQLite** — D. Richard Hipp and team, for a database engine that
  everyone uses and nobody has to think about.
- **Flutter** and **Dart**, for the mobile and web client.
- **OpenSSL**, **libargon2**, **jwt-cpp**, and all the other libraries
  and packages colman (directly or indirectly) depends on.

## Knowledge, standards and open data

- The people who write and maintain standards and specifications —
  ISO C++, the IETF RFCs, MARC 21, ISBN/EAN/GTIN and Code 128 — and
  those who explain them in documentation, books, tutorials, mailing
  lists and Q&A sites.
- **Open Library**, **MusicBrainz**, the **Cover Art Archive**, the
  **Deutsche Nationalbibliothek**, the **Library of Congress** and all
  other institutions and communities who make bibliographic data openly
  accessible.

## Large language models

- To the research community that developed large language models, and —
  not to be forgotten — to all the people whose openly published code,
  documentation and writing these models have learned from.
- In particular to **Anthropic** and its model **Claude**, which, used
  through Claude Code, has been a patient and capable partner in writing
  large parts of colman.  How this collaboration came about and how it
  worked is described in [STORY.md](STORY.md).

## People

- Everyone who has used colman in practice, reported problems, suggested
  improvements and patiently tested new versions.
- And everyone who contributes to free and open source software in any
  way — by writing code, documentation or translations, by reporting
  bugs, by answering questions, or simply by sharing what they know.

Thank you!
